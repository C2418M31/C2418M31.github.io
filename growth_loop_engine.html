Growth Enablement

The Growth
Loop Engine

A perspective on building products that learn.

6 min read

What's often missing isn't better features. It's that they were never meant to talk to each other.

The deeper issue is that features were never looked at holistically. Each one built in isolation, no orchestration between them. So when users arrived at the app, discoverability suffered not because individual features were bad, but because they never formed a system.

That tends to be where the real friction lives. Not in any single feature, but in the space between them.

The Growth Loop Engine is one way to think about that challenge not as a feature list or a roadmap, but as a system where every experiment connects to the last one, feeds the next one, and makes the product smarter with every cycle.

This is systems thinking in practice: every output becomes the input to the next cycle. The engine doesn't just run. It learns.

The Problem

Where the friction tends to live

When a product has no daily-use features, users open it to complete a task and leave. There's no second reason to return. Each missed return is a missed opportunity to deepen the relationship, upsell, or discover what actually drives engagement.

Finding the right engagement hook rarely comes from planning alone. It tends to be discovered through experimentation. And that requires a kind of infrastructure that takes time to build: a way to test quickly, measure precisely, and either double down or cut cleanly.

Without it, learning tends to stop the moment something goes live. It becomes harder to move on from a feature once it's out there, even when the numbers aren't pointing the right way.

The Framework

Four layers, one repeating cycle

The Growth Loop Engine has four components, each built around a different question.

Layer 01

The Signal Layer

Surfaces the real problem not a whiteboard assumption, but friction a real user is experiencing. Draws from customer data, competitive signals, and the experiment archive.

Layer 02

The Filter

Qualifies ideas before anyone touches a keyboard. Three questions: Does it solve a real user problem? Does it help the business? Can it be tested cheaply first?

Layer 03

The Gate

The checkpoint before building starts. Assigns a success metric, confirms the feature connects to something else in the product, and sets a removal date upfront.

Layer 04

The Learning Loop

Where speed becomes knowledge. Every experiment gets measured. Every decision Scale, Iterate, or Kill gets recorded. Nothing is wasted, not even the failures.

A feature that gets killed but taught the team what drives engagement is more valuable than a feature that stays forever and teaches the team nothing.

Going Deeper

The four lenses, in practice

Not every feature drives growth the same way. Some spread the product. Some deepen loyalty. Some show up at exactly the right moment. Some work better in groups. The four lenses map those patterns and make it possible to design experiments with a specific growth outcome in mind.

  • Amplifier
    Organic reach without ad spend. An annual usage summary users share on social the product spreads itself.
  • Loyalty
    Creates a reason to return. A daily streak that rewards consecutive opens makes leaving feel costly.
  • Trigger
    Context-aware, not random. A personalized offer that appears right when a user runs low not pushed on a schedule.
  • Community
    Uses the social graph as a growth mechanism. A group pool where friends collectively contribute for a shared reward.

Features that hit multiple lenses compound. A group challenge users share publicly is both Community and Amplifier. A streak tied to a tiered reward that unlocks shareable content hits all four. The question when designing an experiment isn't just "what should we build?" it's "what kind of growth does this drive, and how will we know?"

The Exit Ramp

The 30/60/90 Removal Rule

One of the harder disciplines in product work is knowing when to let something go. Without a pre-agreed definition of what "not working" looks like, features tend to stay longer than they should.

30
Is anyone using it?

Is the metric trending in the right direction? Early signal check not a verdict, just a read.

60
Is it hitting the target?

Is it helping other features? This is where compound effects start to show or don't.

90
Has it earned its place?

Based on data against a standard everyone already understood not on who built it or how long it took.

The Payoff

What changes when the engine runs

One of the more immediate shifts is in how the conversation gets framed. The question moves from which features to build toward what the team is trying to learn. Every experiment has a hypothesis, a metric, and a deadline. That tends to move decisions closer to evidence.

The deeper shift takes longer but matters more. Every experiment including the ones that get killed gets documented. What was tested, what happened, what was learned. That archive is the team's institutional memory. It's what makes the tenth experiment smarter than the first.

The archive also becomes the foundation for something else entirely. AI can read across every experiment the team has ever run what worked, what didn't, and why and start connecting dots that would take a human months to notice. The tenth experiment doesn't just benefit from nine past learnings. It benefits from AI finding the pattern across all nine.

The goal isn't to ship more. It's to build the shortest path between a hypothesis and a decision and to keep shortening it with every cycle.

A team that learns together builds better than a team that ships alone.
